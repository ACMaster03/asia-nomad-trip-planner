Livhold · Money · design review · round 2 · issues #62 and #59 · Petra's comments folded in

Money starts quiet and earns its cards

Round 2 applies Petra's twenty-two comments from round 1: a "Latest" strip with the last three entries right under the top card and a short confirmation when an entry is saved, the budget line reduced to a percentage and what is left, the One-offs card in two labelled columns, the Plan card leading with its one number and hiding its maths behind a tap, the chart's average in the title line where no bar can cover it, a custom cadence for subscriptions, and shared subscriptions on a shared journey. Every early page now says what will appear later. The frames still replay the Asia journey with the fixture ledger, so every unlock has a date; figures are illustrative and ≈ marks a rounded one.

Decided in #62 and #59, not up for review here
  • The question is asked once per account, because the answer is about the person. Yes opens the full page; No leaves a quiet page with the bookings and an add button. Nothing is deleted either way.
  • Cards unlock per journey as entries arrive. A new journey starts quiet again.
  • Money is per journey. Subscriptions belong to the person; each charge lands in whichever journey is live on the charge date. Yearly is a cadence.
  • Cadence is declared, never inferred. The declaration moves to the moment of categorising: repeat, cadence with monthly prefilled, remind before the charge.
Decided in round 1, from Petra's comments
  • The last three entries sit under the top card, and saving an entry shows a short confirmation; the full ledger stays at the bottom.
  • The budget line says a percentage and what is left; the cap amount lives in its own row, shown from day one.
  • The Plan card leads with the projected total, one line per city, and the maths behind a tap. Planned and paid one-offs are two labelled columns.
  • Subscriptions: "Every month", "Every year" or a custom "every N months"; on a shared journey both travellers see every subscription and get its reminder.
Open, and answered on this page
  • The exact unlock thresholds: proposed in section 6 as a table with the date each one fired on Asia. Seven days for the projection is Patrik's call.
  • What a journey longer than a month shows for Subscriptions before any is declared: a one-line door (section 3), not an empty card.
  • Where No is undone: under your avatar, one switch (section 2).

#62 · the cards a first evening earns · "new" marks what appeared since the quiet page
  • 6The overview appears with the first entry, imports included, so on Asia it was there since the Bangkok stay was charged on 9 July. It shows the one number it can stand behind, spent so far, and the cap bar. The line under the bar says only a percentage and what is left (Petra, round 1: the old "4 500 000 Ft · 3 842 800 Ft left" read as two unrelated numbers); the cap amount lives in its own row further down. Per day says "measuring" with moving dots and its count. Projected total is not there yet: the tile appears after a week of pace (section 5), because a projection from a first-day shop is noise with a currency sign.
  • 7The last three entries sit right under the top card (Petra, round 1: after adding an entry she scrolled to the bottom to check it landed). "Latest" is three rows and a link to the full ledger, and saving an entry shows a short confirmation for two seconds, the dark pill at the bottom. The full ledger stays last (#38), so the cards between them are not buried again.
  • 8One-offs appears because a one-off exists: the backpack was bought on 20 August and there are planned extras. Two labelled columns now, Planned and Paid, both on the right like Bookings (Petra: "why two prices?"): gear planned 205 000, 64 900 paid so far; "nothing paid yet" in amber. On a journey with none, the card waits for the first visa fee.
  • 9Subscriptions on a journey longer than a month is a one-line door, not a card: the name, six words, and ＋, which opens the entry sheet with Subscriptions preselected (section 7). "From home" is gone (Petra): a subscription can start anywhere. It becomes the full card with the first declared subscription. On a journey of a month or less the line is not shown until then.

The budget cap row shows from day one: it is a setting, not a card that needs data. The ledger is present from the first entry, twenty rows, newest first, the "journey starts" band as before.
  • 10Not on this screen, and not hidden behind a menu either: Daily spend, Where it goes and the Plan (Petra: "then where can I see them?"). There is nothing to show yet. They appear by themselves, the chart after three days, the projection after a week, and the last line on the page says so.

Day 9, Tuesday 8 September: a week of pace, so a projection

#62 · the last cards to unlock are the forward-looking ones
  • 14Projected total appears after seven days of pace in a stop. It is the shipped number (stops at your pace, plus bookings, plus one-offs) and, from here on, the one number the page speaks everywhere. Seven days is the proposal: a first week in a city absorbs the arrival shopping; three days does not.
  • 15The Plan card leads with its one number and shows one short line per city (Petra, round 1: "so many informations that I don't even want to read it"). Tapping "How it adds up" opens transport and one-offs. The Add-the-next-stop row is the one forward nudge on the page and points at Trip. "Stay not booked" and "2 legs to book" are amber.
  • 17Tap a city and its sum opens under it, in words (Petra, round 2: "should we write down how this adds up?"): what you already spent here, the nights still to come at your pace, the whole stay already paid. Three lines that add up to the row's number, closed by default, never on the closed card. Bangkok is drawn open here; the other rows are closed. Wording from Petra's round-3 comments: "at your pace" instead of a bare rate, "the whole stay, already paid" instead of a date.
  • 16The budget cap row is there from day one, since it is a setting, not a card that needs data; from here on its line reads "projected uses 77 %" instead of "15 % spent". The bar in the overview measures actual spend against the cap and was there from day 1 too.
  • From day 9 to today the page adds nothing new. The subscriptions line became the card on 14 September, when iCloud charged and was declared (section 7).
A rule proposed with the table below: a card that has appeared on a journey never disappears again on that journey. Thresholds gate the first appearance only, so the page never breathes in and out as entries are edited. Seven days for the projection: Petra agrees; Patrik to confirm.

Categorise as Subscriptions, answer one question

#59 · the charge is the subscription; cadence is still declared, just here instead of in a second form
  • 18The category comes from the name. "Netflix", "Spotify" and "iCloud" are already aliases in the registry, so the chip is preselected. Picking Subscriptions by hand does the same thing. The words "suggested from the name" sit behind the ⓘ now (Petra: too much text).
  • 19The inline block appears the moment Subscriptions is the category and nowhere else: repeat, with monthly prefilled, a yearly for the domain and the annual insurance, and "Other…", which asks for a number of months, every 3 months for the quarterly ones (Petra, round 1). "Doesn't repeat" keeps the entry in the category without declaring anything, for the one-off app purchase. The line names the next charge date, derived from this entry's date, and says where charges go.
  • 20Remind me is on by default, three days before, the shipped lead. It reuses the stay-deadline pipe (#37, migration 40). Off is one tap and is remembered per subscription.
  • Add expense does both things: the row lands in the ledger, dated today, and Netflix is a declared subscription on the card with its next charge on 22 October. No second form, ever. Editing the entry later shows the same block, so a cadence can be corrected where the charge is.
  • A second charge with the same name on a later date is matched to the subscription and offered as "the 22 Oct charge of Netflix?" with one tap to confirm, instead of creating a second subscription. Declined, it is a plain entry.
Model, from #59: the subscription belongs to the person; the row belongs to the journey live on its date. This is what makes the next journey inherit Netflix without inheriting September's charge.

The offer for entries already in the category, once

#59 · "existing entries in the category get the offer once, so the eight to ten on the live trip surface without retyping"
  • 21One card, once, at the top of the page, for every entry already categorised as Subscriptions that is not a declared subscription. Entries with the same name and amount are one row: iCloud has charged twice on Asia and is listed once, with its latest date.
  • 22Every row is prefilled monthly and every row can be changed before the button is pressed; cadence is declared, so the app guesses nothing from the dates, not even the domain's. Here the domain has been switched to yearly by hand.
  • 23Add takes them all; Not now dismisses the card for good. Each entry still gets the inline question whenever it is opened (section 7), so a dismissed offer costs nothing but the shortcut. Netflix, cancelled on 6 September, is added like the others and then marked cancelled on the card, which keeps the August charge (cancel is a state).
  • The reminders default to on with the shipped three-day lead, said in one line under the buttons, and each bell is on the card afterwards, as built in #37.
Decided in round 1 (Petra, the joint card): on a journey you share, both travellers see every subscription and both get its reminder; the offer shows to both, and whoever answers first settles it. Who added a subscription only matters for the next journey: it follows that person, and if the same two travel together again, both have it again.
