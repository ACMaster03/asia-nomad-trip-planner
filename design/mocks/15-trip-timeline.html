Livhold · Trip · design review · round 2 · issues #58 and #63 · Petra's comments folded in

One timeline, and the globe underneath it

Round 2 applies Petra's seventeen comments from round 1: transport as pale-mauve strips so a full list reads at a glance, the budget tick back on the city card, two states instead of three (Idea, Booked), the comfort level behind an ⓘ, an "or" between each deadline date and its chip, the reminder only when there is a free-cancellation date, and the way home no longer naming the last city. Version A and version B now carry labels and a side-by-side table, because the pictures share the same parts and the difference is whether Trip and Map are one screen or two. Sample data is unchanged: the Asia journey on Monday 22 September, day 23, Bangkok night 22.

Decided in #58, not up for review here
  • A place you sleep is a stop; a place you pass through is a via on a leg. A via has no nights, no budget and no check-in expected.
  • Home is a fact about the person, not the journey. Every journey starts and ends there without being asked.
  • A stop holds one or more stays; a gap between them is a warning, not an error. Two stays in one stop may not overlap each other.
  • Extras has left for Money as One-offs (#39). The tabs Stops / Stays / Transport / Extras go.
  • Nights math, "Bangkok, night 22", booked / not booked, cancel-by reminders and the plan → ledger import keep working unchanged.
Decided in round 1, from Petra's comments
  • The budget tick stays on the city card: fastest, reversible, and a faded card shows a maybe-stop at a glance.
  • Two states everywhere, Idea and Booked. A price typed on an Idea counts as the estimate. Shortlist is gone.
  • The comfort level leaves the card and sits in the editor behind an ⓘ. The summary line is just the nights.
  • The way home is a dashed "not planned yet" leg into the home node; no card names the last city as the last stop.
Open, and decided on a phone

#58 · one page in date order, no tabs; the rail on the left is the journey
  • 1Home is the first and the last node, drawn hollow, with no nights and no card to edit. Where you live is set once under your avatar (Account) and reused by every journey. The globe's ⌂ marker already exists.
  • 2A leg is the strip between two stops and the only place transport is entered. Legs are pale mauve, the app's transport colour, and stops are white cards, so a full list still reads at a glance (Petra, round 1). Its from, to and date come from the stops on either side, so the flight is never typed as "transport or extra". A booked leg is solid and shows its price and paid state; via Shanghai · 14 h 35 is the connection, a place you pass through, drawn on the globe as a dogleg with the hours on it.
  • 3A stop card is what the Stops card is today, plus its stays underneath: the tick, the name, the dates, the progress bar for the stop you are in, then one row per stay with its nights, its money state and its total. The comfort level is gone from the card, it meant nothing next to a paid stay, and lives in the editor. An upcoming charge reads "card charged on 29 Sep" in amber; a paid stay reads "paid 9 Jul" in green. "+ Add stay" lives on the stop, not on a separate page.
  • 4An unbooked leg is dashed and says so; "+ Add transport" opens the leg editor (section 6). The rail itself goes dashed there too, so the gap in the plan is visible from across the room.
  • 5Two stays in Da Nang would be two rows; here there is one and a gap. "No bed 30 Nov → 13 Dec" is the amber row, a warning, and tapping it opens Add stay with those dates already in (section 5). The overlap warning stays per stop: two stays in Da Nang may not overlap each other.
  • 6A stop is inserted at the rail, between any two nodes; the last "+ Add stop" is always there, above the way home. The way home is a dashed "not planned yet" leg into the home node, which says "Home again · 30 Apr". The last city you added is not necessarily the last one of the journey (Petra, round 1), so no card names it as such.
  • 7The journey picker is the title (#21: "current journey", chosen from your journeys in the Trip header, not in Settings). Behind the gear: dates, currency, cap, travel partners, sharing, delete or leave.
  • The summary line is just the nights: the stops and the date home are already on the page. Stays and legs have two states, Idea and Booked; a price typed on an Idea counts as the estimate. Extras is gone: visas, insurance and gear are One-offs on Money.
Decided in round 1 (Petra): the tick stays on the city card. It is the fastest way to take a stop out of the budget, it is reversible, and a faded card shows a maybe-stop at a glance.
Copy diet applied (#65): the four paragraphs that opened Stops, Stays, Transport and Extras are gone. Nothing on this screen explains itself; the one sentence left is on the home node.

Add stay: the deadlines are asked when the stay is added

#60 · on the live trip no deadline reminder ever fired, because no stay had a cancel-by date
  • 18Opened from the amber gap row, so the stop and the dates are already in. Opened from "+ Add stay" the dates default to the stop's dates, or to the nights still uncovered if there is a stay already.
  • 19The two dates the reminders need, each with an "or" and an explicit no (Petra, round 1): you either type until when it is free to cancel, or you say there is no free cancellation; you either type the charge date, or you say the card is charged at check-in. A stay that was never asked and a stay with no cancellation window used to look the same, and both produced no reminder. Now a blank means "still to enter" and shows as "deadlines not set" on the stop card.
  • 20The reminder switch appears only once a free-cancellation date is filled in (Petra, round 1); with no free cancellation there is nothing to remind. It is on by default and names its date, 3 days before, as a card on Home. It is the derived deadline reminder that already exists, born here instead of on a Reminders page. The manual reminders list goes to Labs (#64).
  • The Booked chip is what makes Money import the stay on its charge date, as today. Idea stays keep the deadline fields hidden; they appear the moment Booked is chosen.
  • The second door (#60): Trip settings gets a Deadlines row listing every cancel-by and charge date on the journey, whether or not any is due. A feature that only appears once it has content needs a door that does not.
Decided in round 1 (Petra): nothing about forwarding booking emails until that feature exists.

Transport on the leg, and the via that is not a stop

#58 · "Add transport" lives on the leg, between the two stop cards
  • 21From, to and date are the leg's, taken from the stops on either side, and are not fields. The only date typed is a departure time. Change the stop's Leave date and this leg moves with it (section 4).
  • 22The connection is optional and small: one link under the flight fields, shown for flights only, no explanation. Petra's question in round 1, is it needed at all: not for the money. It exists so the globe can draw the plane via Shanghai for the family, and so nobody adds Shanghai as a city and breaks the night count. A via has no nights, no budget, no check-in expected. If nobody uses it, it goes.
  • 23Booked reveals the charge date and the leg draws solid on the rail and on the globe; Money imports it on that date as today ("from booking"). An Idea leg draws dashed and forecasts with a price if one is typed, nothing if not.
  • Delete is gone from this sheet on purpose: a leg cannot be deleted, only emptied, because two stops always have a leg between them. "Remove transport" sits under a rule once something is booked.
Decided in round 1 (Petra): two states, Idea and Booked, for legs and stays alike. Idea with no price forecasts nothing, Idea with a price forecasts that price, Booked is money owed. Shortlist was a third state between them and confused more than it helped.

Decide on a phone

What to look at before choosing

  1. Open this page on your phone and tap the three sheets in section 2 until each height feels right or wrong. The strip is the thing you will see most: is one line enough, or does it want the progress bar?
  2. Cold open time. The globe today loads the three.js bundle and its textures on every Map visit. If Trip is opened ten times a day, that cost is paid ten times, unless the instance is kept alive across the tab. Time it on mobile data before deciding.
  3. Thumb versus globe. A drag meant for the list that spins the planet instead is the classic failure of this pattern. The sheet has to win every gesture that starts on it.
  4. The editing day. Adding three stays and two legs, with the globe alive behind the sheet: does the phone get warm, does the battery dip. Variant B has none of this cost and pays for it with a fuller tab bar and two places to edit a stop.
Either way, section 1, 4, 5 and 6 are the build. The timeline, the stop editor with its stays, the stay form with the deadlines, and transport on the leg do not depend on the sheet decision. Only the shell around them does. If the phone test is inconclusive, build the timeline as a page first (variant B without the cross-links) and put the sheet on it afterwards: the sheet is a container, the timeline is the content.
Not in this mock, on purpose: the between-journeys Home and the journey picker's list (#21), the Share button (#61), the copy pass on the rest of the app (#65). Side trips are parked (#57).
