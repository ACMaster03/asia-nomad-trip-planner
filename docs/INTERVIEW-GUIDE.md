# Interview guide — long-journey travellers

For twenty-minute conversations with strangers, in person or on a call. Built
on one rule: **talk about their life, not your idea.** Livhold is not mentioned
until the last three minutes, and only if they ask or you are asking for a
commitment. Who to find and where is in `docs/MARKETING.md`.

## Seven rules

1. **Past and present, never future.** "How did you do it last time" is
   evidence. "Would you use an app that…" is worthless; people are polite.
2. **Hunt the workaround.** A spreadsheet, a group chat, screenshots, a Notes
   file. A workaround is pain they are already paying for with their time.
3. **Ask for the number.** "Do you know what last month cost you?" If they
   know it, ask how. If they don't, ask how they would find out. Both answers
   matter.
4. **The last time, not the usual.** "Tell me about the last time you booked
   the next place" gets a story. "How do you usually book" gets a theory.
5. **Write down their words.** The exact phrases. They become the copy.
6. **Compliments are nothing. Commitments are evidence.** "Sounds cool" means
   nothing. "Send me the link", "can I follow your journey", "talk to my
   friend" mean something.
7. **Shut up.** After a question, wait. The second answer is the true one.

## The ask (in person or a message)

> We're a couple from Hungary, on the road since [month]. We're building
> something for people on long journeys, and I'd love twenty minutes to hear
> how you handle yours. No pitch, I just want to understand how people like
> you plan and keep track. Coffee's on us.

## The script (about twenty minutes)

**Opening, two minutes.** How long have you been on the road? Where before
this, where next? Who's with you? Is there an end date, or not yet?

**Planning, five minutes.**
- How did you end up in this city? Walk me through it.
- Where did the options live while you were deciding: tabs, notes, a sheet,
  a chat?
- How far ahead do you know the next place? What decides when you leave?
- (With a partner) Who does what? How do you keep each other in sync?
- What went wrong the last time you planned a move?

**Money, five minutes.**
- Do you know what last month cost you? How do you know?
- What do you use, and since when? What did you use before that?
- Has money ever changed the plan: leaving earlier, skipping a country,
  staying longer because it was cheap?
- How do you two split or share it? (if a couple)

**People at home, five minutes.**
- How do your parents know where you are right now?
- How often do you tell them, and where: a call, a group chat, Instagram?
- Is there anything you tell your mum that you would never post?
- Has anyone at home ever said they don't hear enough from you?

**Close, three minutes.**
- What's the most annoying part of all this?
- Then the commitment asks, in order, stop when one lands:
  "Can I show you something in a week?" · "Can I send you a link to follow our
  journey, so you see what we mean?" · "Who else do you know who's doing this,
  or about to?"

## After each conversation, five lines

Write these within an hour, while the words are fresh:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Who | first name, age bracket, solo or couple, working or not |
| Journey | how long so far, how long planned, end date or open, cities so far |
| Workarounds | plan: … · money: … · family: … (the actual tools) |
| Hottest pain | which of the three, in their words |
| Commitment | none · show me · follow link · intro to … |

**ICP fit** = a month or more, several cities, at least two of the three
workarounds present, and at least one person at home who wants updates.
Two of three workarounds is strong. Zero workarounds means they have no
problem, whatever they said.

## The watching test (after five interviews)

Send the link, say nothing, watch them set up a journey. Note every place they
stop or ask. Afterwards, one question per stop: "what did you expect to happen
here?" Never explain during. Five of these before 31 October.
