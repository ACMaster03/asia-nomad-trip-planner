# Adversarial Critique — Option A (PWA + Capacitor evolution)

Verified against the repo at `/Users/grohmannpatrik/Claude/Projects/Ázsia nomad trip planning/asia-nomad-planner`. Codebase claims in the design are mostly accurate (migrations 01–05 exist as described; `trip_invites`/`trip_members` have zero references in `product/src` — genuinely dormant; `prefetchTripScreen()` + HydrationBoundary pattern confirmed on all pages; `proxy.ts` is the Next 16 middleware replacement; no manifest/SW exists; no tests exist anywhere). The serious problems are in sequencing, the sharing security model, and free-tier/offline assumptions.

---

## BLOCKER findings

**B1. M0 ships the data-loss amplifier; the safety comes in M2.** The design adds the TanStack IndexedDB persister (offline reads) in M0 but the `rev`/`updated_at` write-guard only in M2. Today, every page load server-prefetches fresh state, so the stale-write window is seconds. With a restored offline cache, `useTripMutation` (`product/src/lib/trips/useTripMutation.ts:21`) reads the trip from the query cache and `writeState` (`queries.ts:50`) overwrites the **whole** `state` document unconditionally — a phone opened after three offline days silently clobbers the partner's week of edits. The persister without the guard makes LWW *worse* than the current app, during the exact period two editors are most active.
**Fix:** the guard is ~20 lines (`.eq('updated_at', expected)` + retry/conflict toast in `writeState`/`writeLedger`). Land it in M0 *before or with* the persister. Non-negotiable ordering.

**B2. The schedule doesn't survive contact with the trip date.** `defaultState.ts` sets `startDate: '2026-10-01'`. Today is July 10 → **11.7 weeks to departure**. M0–M2 is 10–11 person-weeks *full-time*, with zero slack, zero soak, and no dogfood window — and M0 front-loads 3–4 weeks of cosmetics (destination palettes, `dark:` sweep, UX polish notes) ahead of everything trip-critical. If the owner is not full-time on this (he has a job), Live mode lands mid-trip or never.
**Fix:** resequence by trip-criticality, not by layer: week 1–2 = write-guard + stay-deadline alerts (email-first via Resend — works on iOS day one, no push infra needed); week 3–6 = check-ins + `/follow/[code]`; theming and the owner's UX-notes batch ship *during* the trip — it's a website, deploys are free. Add a mandatory weekend-trip dogfood of the check-in flow before Oct 1.

## MAJOR findings

**M1. The sharing security model is broken today, and migration 06 understates the repair.** Verified in `supabase/schema.sql:212` / `migrations/01:11`: `trips_update` policy is `using (can_access_trip(id))` with no `WITH CHECK` and no column protection, and `can_access_trip` (schema.sql:177) **ignores role**. Consequences: (a) a `viewer` member can UPDATE `trips.state` — the viewer role is decorative; (b) any member can reassign `owner` to themselves (new row still passes `can_access_trip`) — ownership hijack. Harmless with two trusting users; a live hole the moment "family sharing" adds members. The design calls these tables "already the right shape — migration 06 wires them into UI." Shape yes, policies no.
**Fix:** migration 06 must ship a role-aware `can_edit_trip()`, rewrite `trips_update` with proper `USING`/`WITH CHECK`, and guard the `owner` column (trigger, mirroring the existing `guard_profile_admin_flag` pattern in 03-catalogue.sql). Budget it explicitly.

**M2. Two sharing mechanisms are conflated, creating a privacy surprise.** M1 "family sharing = wire trip_members viewers into Settings" gives family the **full app**, including Money/Ledger — RLS is row-level and the entire budget lives inside `trips.state`. The design itself says (§3.2) the follow page must never expose raw state because it contains budgets — then ships budget-visible viewer sharing one milestone earlier under the label "family sharing."
**Fix:** define the taxonomy now: `trip_members` = co-editors only (the girlfriend); family = `share_links` followers, no account, sanitized RPC projection. Either move follow links into M1 or relabel M1's feature as "add co-editor."

**M3. The free-tier cost claim is factually wrong where it matters: scheduling.** Vercel Hobby allows **2 cron jobs, once per day each**, and prohibits commercial use. The design needs 3 daily crons (fx/weather/advisories) *plus* sub-daily scanning of `notification_queue` for cancellation-deadline pushes — impossible on Hobby. "~$0/mo through M2" is false as architected.
**Fix:** move scheduling into Supabase (pg_cron + pg_net/Edge Functions — genuinely free-tier) and keep Vercel routes for request-shaped work only; or accept Vercel Pro ($20/mo) from M1. Either is fine; the design must pick one.

**M4. "Queue mutations in IndexedDB via TanStack persisted mutations" cannot work with the current mutation design.** `useTripMutation`'s mutation *variables* are closures — `(cur: TripState) => TripState` — which are unserializable; persisted/resumable mutations also require a default `mutationFn` on the QueryClient for post-reload resume. The offline-write story as written is a non-starter without redesigning the write path into serializable ops (JSON Patch or scoped section-replace), which is real work the estimate omits.
**Fix:** descope: offline **reads** for the plan (persister + guard), offline **writes only for check-ins** (they're serializable rows with `created_at` — trivially queueable and conflict-free, and they're the only thing you actually write while on a mountain). Offline plan-editing is a v2 problem.

**M5. The iOS native offline story has a hidden dependency the design never names.** In the remote-URL Capacitor shell, service workers in WKWebView only run with **App-Bound Domains** configured (`WKAppBoundDomains`, `limitsNavigationsToAppBoundDomains`); without it, "offline is handled at the service-worker layer (shared with the PWA)" is false on iOS — cold-start offline is a white screen, which is itself a review rejection (2.1) before 4.2 even comes up. Also, "Guideline 4.2 risk is identical in both sub-modes" oversells: Capacitor's own docs discourage `server.url` in production, and a remote shell is optically worse in review (network spinners, blank offline) than a bundled build. The static-export fallback is called "documented" but its cost (reworking every `page.tsx` off `prefetchTripScreen`) is unbudgeted — if the fallback fires, M4 blows past 3–4 weeks.
**Fix:** add App-Bound Domains config + native offline splash to M4 scope; stand up a CI job that attempts `output: 'export'` early (cheap, continuously measures how far the fallback has drifted).

**M6. The static fallback app is an armed writer that bypasses every safeguard.** Verified in `js/cloud.js:79-82`: `cloudPush` updates `{ state, ledger }` **together** in one payload — it clobbers both columns, defeats the product app's careful state/ledger column separation (`queries.ts:36-49`), will strip any new fields (`state.rev`) from the document, and ignores the M2 write-guard entirely. "Freeze, eventually delete" is not a plan; one nostalgic open tab of the old app mid-trip undoes a day of edits.
**Fix:** at M0, make the static app read-only (gate `cloudPush` off) or remove its Supabase config. One hour of work; do it first.

**M7. No staging, no migration tooling, no backups — and migrations 06–10 rewrite RLS on the live table.** Migrations are SQL-editor paste files; Supabase free tier has no PITR and no backups; M3+ schema changes land *while the couple is travelling and depending on the app*. A botched `can_access_trip` replacement locks both travelers out of their own trip from a hostel wifi. Constraint (3) says breaking the working app is unacceptable — the design has no mechanism enforcing that.
**Fix:** second free Supabase project as staging + Supabase CLI migrations + a manual dump before every prod migration; freeze prod RLS changes to a pre-departure window; additive-only tables during the trip. Also note: zero automated tests exist — a minimal RLS test script (even psql assertions run against staging) for migrations 06–07 is cheap insurance.

## MINOR findings

- **"The app currently has zero server-only code paths" is false** — `app/auth/callback/route.ts` and `auth/confirm/route.ts` are route handlers. The defensible claim is "no server-held secrets." Cosmetic, but it's stated as a factual premise for the `/api` layer.
- **Realtime revocation gap:** broadcast on `trip:{id}:live` means a revoked follower who ever loaded the page keeps the channel name (trip UUID) and can subscribe forever. Use share-code-scoped channel names rotated on revoke, or Realtime private-channel authorization.
- **Media read path for anon followers is unspecified:** `/api/media/sign` covers uploads only; the public follow page needs signed GET URLs (RPC or route) or a public bucket with unguessable paths. Small, but it's on the M2 critical path.
- **Cost claims fray at the edges:** Mux's "free tier" is effectively trial credit — budget Cloudflare Stream (~$5/1k min) as the realistic alternative; AeroDataBox free quota is trial-scale and "plane just landed" pings require polling (paid tier); Google Play's $25 is unmentioned. None architectural.
- **Theming estimate is roughly right, verified:** ~220 hardcoded color utilities measured (45 `dark:`, 17 `teal-*`, 159 `neutral-*` across 24 files) — the sweep is days, not a week. The real risk is design, not engineering: 4 dominant colors extracted from photos almost never yield accessible UI tokens in both light and dark; constrain palettes to accent + surface-tint over a fixed neutral scale.
- **The multi-trip refactor ripples further than "one queries.ts change":** `tk.activeTrip` is the cache identity baked into `useTripScreen`, `useTripMutation` (line 21), `useLedgerMutation`, `SettingsClient`, and `prefetch.ts`. Contained, but it must land before new consumers (Live tab) are built on the old key — the M1 placement is correct, just don't let it slip past M2.
- **Magic link in the shell is worse than "miserable":** PKCE stores the code verifier in the requesting context's storage; the emailed link opens in Safari/Mail's browser, a different context — the exchange *fails*, not just annoys. The design's fix (OTP code + OAuth + Apple Sign-In) is right; the severity framing is wrong, which matters because it's also flaky for the PWA today.

## What the design gets right (verified)

The core bets hold: check-ins as normalized rows outside `trips.state` is the correct fork point; the follow-page-as-URL insight is genuinely the strongest product argument for this option; the SECURITY DEFINER RPC pattern for anon follow mirrors the existing, well-built `is_admin()`/`can_access_trip()` patterns in 03-catalogue.sql; the dynamic `catalogue_fields` rendering claim is real (verified `FieldRenderer` + registry); reuse claims are honest — the domain layer is clean and the mutation pattern does extend; foreground-only, event-based location framing is the right call; the Expo-hedge discipline (DOM-free `src/lib`, no direct `@capacitor/*` imports in components) is cheap and correct.

## VERDICT: SOUND-WITH-FIXES

The architecture is right for this owner, this product, and this business model; the plan as sequenced would damage the one thing it must protect — the real trip's data and deadline. The three fixes that matter most:

1. **Resequence around Oct 1 and close the write-safety hole first:** rev/updated_at guard + disarm the static app's writer in week 1 (before the offline persister, not two milestones after); deadline alerts email-first; check-ins + follow page by week 6; theming and polish ship mid-trip.
2. **Fix the sharing model in migration 06 for real:** role-aware `can_edit_trip()`, `WITH CHECK` + owner-column guard on `trips_update`, and a hard taxonomy of members=co-editors vs family=follow-links — so "family sharing" never means "family reads the ledger."
3. **Replace the free-tier fictions with a working ops plan:** scheduling on Supabase pg_cron/Edge Functions (or budget Vercel Pro), offline writes descoped to serializable check-in rows, App-Bound Domains + offline splash budgeted into M4, and a staging Supabase project + CLI migrations before any RLS change touches the live trip.
