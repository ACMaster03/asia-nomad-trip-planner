# Competitors — what people say about them

One section per app that people name. Only what they say, with the source ID. What the
app's own website says does not go here; we can read that any time. What we think it
means goes in `takeaways.md`.

The second half, "The non-apps", is for the things people use instead of any app:
email, a calendar, a spreadsheet, paper. In S-01 those beat every app.

## TripIt

- What it is, as people describe it: an app that builds your trip from the booking confirmation emails you forward to it. Free, with a paid version (Pro).
- Who uses it, from the threads: people who travel often, some for 15 or 20 years; one planning a multi-leg trip (S-01, r/femaletravelers). The most named app in S-01, about 18 people.
- Praised for:
  - "Works flawlessly , free , used it for years" — S-01 [4]
  - "Forward your confirmation emails to plans@tripit.com and it builds your trips for you and can sync to your local calendar." — S-01 [5]
  - "Also nice to see all the old trip/memories in one app" — S-01 [3]
  - "really came through for me when I was planning a multi-leg trip" — S-01 [2]
  - "Sooo easy to organize trips with it!" — S-01 [1]
  - "keeps everything straight for me, including the address which launches Google maps when selected. Also includes the business phone number!" — S-01 [1]
  - "I even get updates of terminals my flights leave from. So easy to use" — S-01 [2]
  - "I have probably close to 15 years of travel in there." — S-01 [1]
- Complained about:
  - "except when there might be adjustments to travel plans it can get a bit confusing" — S-01 [3]
  - "tripit and wanderlog both pull stuff from your email but sometimes miss bookings so double check" — S-01 [24]
  - one person left it for Google Sheets, to open the plan on every device — S-01 [1]
- Price people mention: free and paid versions; a "big Black Friday offer" on the paid one; one would pay full price "for the next time I have a lot going on"; one paid to attach photos of confirmation codes and get airport maps, and says the free version is enough unless you fly every few days — S-01
- Sources: S-01

## Wanderlog

- What it is, as people describe it: a planning app you can forward booking emails to, with a journal.
- Who uses it, from the threads: people planning a trip of a few weeks; about 8 in S-01, several new to it.
- Praised for:
  - "It lets you forward emails with booking confirmations, etc. I love it." — S-01 [7]
  - "Wanderlog has made keeping track of everything so so easy. My favourite travel app" — S-01 [2]
  - "Wanderlog has a journal option where you can write about your experiences as you travel. We are going on a 3 week trip in Italy soon and it has been so helpful in planning." — S-01 [3]
  - "I've just started using Wanderlog for next week's trip and it's pretty dang handy." — S-01 [1]
- Complained about:
  - "sometimes miss bookings so double check" — S-01 [24], said of TripIt and Wanderlog together
- Price people mention: none
- Sources: S-01

## TripAdvisor's itinerary builder

- What it is, as people describe it: a list inside TripAdvisor where you mark what is booked and type the booking number.
- Who uses it, from the threads: one person, next to Gmail stars and a paper planner.
- Praised for:
  - "I mark which activities/restaurants/hotels have been booked, and it has a field to enter the booking number." — S-01 [16]
- Complained about: nothing
- Price people mention: none
- Sources: S-01

## The non-apps

What people use instead of a travel app. In S-01 these are the majority.

### Email: folders, stars, search, snooze

- About 22 of 80 in S-01. A folder per trip is the most common single answer.
- "Folders in email" — S-01 [18]
- "I star every booking confirmation in my Gmail." — S-01 [16]
- "I use the "snooze" feature in Gmail and set the email to pop back into my inbox the morning before I need that info." — S-01 [2]
- Where it fails: "sometimes the words I use to find the email are not within the email so it takes forever to find the email Im looking for" — S-01 [1]

### A calendar (Google Calendar, the phone's)

- About 14 in S-01, often next to email folders.
- "I use Google calendar to input all the details and attach any confirmations or tickets to each individual event" — S-01 [5]
- "with time adjusted for time zone so it's accurate, and confirmation number and links in the notes" — S-01 [1]
- Where it fails: "one time i spent all this time doing that and it didn't save … frustrating and stressful because I was on the spot" — S-01 [1]

### A spreadsheet or a document (Google Sheets, Docs, Word, Excel)

- About 12 spreadsheets and 9 documents in S-01. The heavier the trip, the more likely.
- "i just use a ggl spread sheet per trip lol, paste confirmation numbers and links in as i book." — S-01 [24]
- "Google Docs for each trip." — S-01 [11]
- "Really nice that you can embed links to keep everything connected but looking neat and organized." — S-01 [2]
- "I like to be able to access my information across multiple devices so I don't want to get locked into an app" — S-01 [1]

### Paper

- About 12 in S-01, many of them also with something digital as a backup.
- "I just write it down on paper, I've always got a little booklet with me" — S-01 [10]
- "I can physically see how many days I have in each location which helps with planning and considering rest days." — S-01 [5]
- "until I literally hand write it all out, I don't feel ready to travel." — S-01 [4]

### Screenshots and files on the phone, for offline

- About 7 in S-01.
- "Landing at a new airport/country does not always guarantee internet straight away." — S-01 [6]
- "Then I don't have to worry about having internet" — S-01 [4]

### An AI chat (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini)

- 3 in S-01, all at one upvote or below; they paste screenshots and let the chat build the list.
- "I just send in screenshots of my trips and bookings and it compiles them for me." — S-01 [1]
