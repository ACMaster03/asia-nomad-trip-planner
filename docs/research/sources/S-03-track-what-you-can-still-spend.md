## The post

> "I'm curious how people handle this once they're actually traveling for a while.
>
> Do you mostly: keep a rough number in your head / use Notes or a spreadsheet / use your
> bank/credit card apps / manually log everything in an app / not track it closely at all
>
> I'm especially curious about the friction side of it. Does manually entering every
> purchase get annoying enough that you stop? And if purchases could show up automatically
> from the cards you're using, would that feel easier or would you rather keep everything

>

> pre-booked things, currency conversion, or something else?
>
> I'm working on this problem and trying to understand what people actually do before I
> make assumptions."

## What people said

Word for word. Upvotes in brackets.

### An app, updated daily

> "I use multiple cards and cash so I use an app called TravelSpend - tracks everything - I
> don't find it annoying to update it once a day" [2]

> "The - - makes me wonder if this is an ad" [2] (a reply to the one above)

### A rough number, then a look at the bank

> "I don't track it very rigidly. I basically have a rough daily budget in my head for
> regular expenses.
>
> It's not a hard limit where I stop spending once I hit that number. Some days I spend
> less, some days more, and I just try to keep the average around that amount.
>
> I also keep a separate amount in mind for bigger or extraordinary expenses, like tours or
> things I pay for once but use for a month, because I don't think it makes much sense to
> count those as part of one day's spending.
>
> Then I check my bank account to see how much I actually spent over the month. So far,
> I've managed to stay pretty close to my overall budget without tracking every single
> purchase." [1]

> "Rough daily number plus a look at the bank balance once a week beats logging every
> coffee, for me. The daily entry is what makes people drop the app, not the maths." [1]
> (from the self-promotion comment, see below)

### A spreadsheet at the start, then the card bill

> "We used a spreadsheet for the first couple of months, which was pretty tedious to be
> honest. Once we could see that we were sticking to the budget we stopped doing it.
> Everything goes on the credit card so we could see each month if the habits were starting
> to slip as we knew what the overall bill should look like. I do think it's a pretty good
> idea to keep a close eye on it as you start, even if it is just to check if your intial
> budget is realistic." [1]

### The bank does it

> "I put it in a separate account so I can see what is left. Anything already designated
> stays in a different account (hotels, tickets)." [1]

> "I use my bank app, has all my currencies and pockets and how much I've allotted each
> month" [1]

### Card import, and what breaks a budget

> "I would always prefer the automated card tracking. Manually logging every coffee is very
> exhausting, especially for long trips.
>
> And the biggest budget destroyers are cash transactions and hidden currency conversion
> fees. All these seem to vanish from our budget trackers." [0]

> "Pre-booked things bend a budget worst, a deposit paid months back with the balance due
> mid trip." [1] (self-promotion comment; it goes on to name its own product, dEssence, and
> link to it)

### The poster, replying

> "would you prefer purchases to stay manual, or would automatic card imports be useful as
> long as you could review/correct them? Automatic card tracking seems like the best but not
> too many options for it :/" [1]

## Needs heard

- N-14 — Purchases come in from the cards; typing every coffee is what makes people stop
- N-15 — A rough daily number and a monthly check, not every purchase
- N-16 — Big one-off costs kept apart from the day-to-day spending
- N-17 — Cash and currency fees should not vanish from the total
- N-18 — Watch closely at the start, to learn if the budget is realistic, then relax
- N-19 — Pre-booked costs with money still due mid-trip
